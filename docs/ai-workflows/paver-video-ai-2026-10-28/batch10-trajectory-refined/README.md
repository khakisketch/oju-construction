## Experimental design and guide
`start.png` is existing RealVisXL-generated reference-5 (1024×640); its angled grassy paver surface provides a more oblique context than batch 9. It is synthetic, and not a rejected Qwen edit. Resized 640×400, `guide.png` was drawn and inspected **before inference**: six red start points lie visibly inside the same recognizable block; eight cyan static anchors lie outside it. The guide's red 16px lines show the larger case; the smaller case uses 8px. They are illustrations of inputs, not model output. `guide-source.py` reproduces this diagram; exact frame-by-frame coordinates in both graphs are the authority. Both guides prescribe y increasing quadratically over 49 frames, 0 horizontal shift. Same seed, prompt and eight anchors; only trajectory displacement differs.

Downward screen movement was chosen as a tentative visible-direction hypothesis for this oblique view, not a calibrated projection of gravity. Without camera pose, geometry and depth, 2D tracks cannot prove real-world vertical motion or rigid-body constraints. Conditioning can be ignored or hallucinate content and does not clamp the endpoint.

Results `measure.json`: case 8 target best shift (dx,dy)=(1,2), residual MSE 169.12 vs 702.73 at zero; background (0,-1), 115.66 vs 161.21. Case 16 target (-1,14), residual MSE 1052.99 vs 3277.38; background (0,0), 65.80. Units pixels and squared 8-bit grayscale intensity. **These are patch similarity estimates, not tracked paver displacement:** the target is low-texture and is occluded/changed by the generated object; large residuals, changing lighting and morphology invalidate a rigid-motion interpretation. The estimates independently quantify image changes, not true physical lowering or faithful adherence to the prescribed tracks.
